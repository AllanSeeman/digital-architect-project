Malachi (which may or may not be the actual name of the prophet) is organized into SIX DISPUTATIONS… each one is Israel making a complaint and God rebuking that complaint with warning and/or divine instruction… c. Just before Malachi 3, Israel cries out, “Where is the God of Justice?” the title of this sermon… So, where is he? Why doesn’t his stormcloud rest over the temple? 3 Why doesn’t he show up and right the wrongs of the wicked? How long will he wait to do his justice and restore his people? d. Does that sound familiar? Have you ever wondered where God is in the midst of your own circumstances? I know I have… About 15 years ago, I was wondering how God could do this to me, or allow this to happen, and I wanted to know where he was. I had volunteered to go to Afghanistan in the place of an acquaintance in my military unit. He was on track to deploy to fight the Taliban, but his father suddenly got diagnosed with terminal cancer. It didn’t take long to think about what I should do. I told the chief of my section I would like to go in his place, and I did. While I was there, I was working the AM shift (from midnight until noon), launching and recovering aircraft mostly. Air dropping weapons and ammunition, transporting soldiers and airmen, and delivering life-saving blood to the most forward medical facilities and front lines. One night before I woke up to go to work, the Chief and the Captain came knocking on my plywood door. They said I needed to turn over my weapons and come with them to make a phone call. It was one of the hardest things they ever had to do and they didn’t even know what the call was about. All they knew was I had received a call from the American Red Cross while I was in a war zone. That could only mean one thing… the death of a close family member. It turned out, it was my mom. I couldn’t think of a worse person to lose in that moment. This woman who had cared for me, and loved me unconditionally since birth, I was so alone. And I wanted to know why… where was God… how could he allow this… I wanted the God of justice to end the war in Afghanistan, to take away my friend’s dad’s cancer, and to bring back my mom. 7. It was the first-in unit to “prepare the way” for the big Air Force, or for the contingency operation that was about to take place. Most people didn’t notice, but this man was a graduate from the same seminary I have the honor of graduating from later this year… and he slipped in the prophecy from Malachi… “Prepare the Way” became our squadron motto. c. To prepare the way before the Lord means the time or place, the setting, wasn’t ready for his presence… and the fact that he is sending his messenger means the people weren’t ready… they needed to hear something before he comes. POINT #2 “Who is the second messenger?” “And the Messenger of the covenant in whom you delight, behold, he is coming, says the Lord of hosts.” (Malachi 3:1c) a. The Messenger of the covenant… who might that be? Well, if we look briefly into the Old Covenants we see the Word of the Lord, the Angel of the Lord, and the Name of the Lord… these are ways to describe YHWH coming to his people directly. However, we know our God as a Triune God. Nothing is done by one person of the Trinity in secret from the others, but it does seem that in their relation to one another, we can see a pattern of the Father sending the Son, and the Spirit. This One who is coming, sounds a lot like the One who brought the old covenant to God’s people, and he is coming to renew that covenant for New Covenant believers… this Messenger, friends, is Jesus. We see him come immediately following John the Baptist who we know was preparing the way for him. b. Most intriguing to me is this Angel of the Lord figure who shows up at various points in Scripture and then speaks as if he himself is the LORD speaking. He has authority. Perhaps even the same figure from Joshua’s encounter before the battle of Jericho. It’s important to note, this word Angel, Mal’ak in the Hebrew, and Angelos, in the Greek, is not a word that describes a type of being as much as it is a word that describes a function… the role the person is playing, the duty they have, is a messenger. We see seraphim and cherubim not described as angels but other spiritual beings in the heavens and in the throne room of God… In Joshua’s story, a man comes before Joshua, the Bible calls him “sar” in Hebrew, which could be translated prince, captain, or representative of the King. When Joshua bows down, the Commander or Prince of the Lord’s Army does not tell him to get up like ordinary messengers of God do. He accepts his worship and even says where you are standing is holy ground! DISPLAY JOSHUA 5:14-15 6 This is the same thing the Angel of the Lord said to Moses from the burning bush. “Take your sandals off your feet, for the place on which you are standing is holy ground.” DISPLAY EXODUS 3:2-5 c. What’s the point? You might be saying… the point is, these messengers from the Old Testament are many times, God himself manifesting in some way to intervene in the lives of his people. This is exactly what scholars see happening in this prophecy from Malachi about the messenger of the covenant. Jesus. So, did Jesus come to his temple to bring justice? Not yet, right? Because all have sinned and fallen short of the glory of God (DISPLAY ROMANS 3:23) and the wages of sin is death (DISPLAY ROMANS 6:23)… this will be his justice for sinners in the final days.
